Tracks & Example Problems

Safe and healthy communities

Examples: Medicine reminder for a grandparent, clean-water checklist, first-aid game

Farming and local livelihoods

Examples: Crop-price record, a small seller’s profit calculator, pest identification guide

Climate and environment

Examples: Water-tank overflow alert, waste-sorting game, heat-safety awareness tool

Better learning at school

Examples: Local-language maths game, attendance tracker, science model with sensors

Inclusion and girls’ safety

Examples: Tool for a classmate with a disability, safe-route map, story game about rights

AI for good (optional, Class 8-9)

Examples: Simple image or sound classifier, plus a discussion of where it could go wrong

Judging Parameters

Judges evaluate each project on five parameters (1–10 each). Thinking about the problem is valued over technical polish.

1. Problem Understanding

Clarity and depth of understanding of the real-life problem (Meri Samasya).

2. Solution Relevance & Impact

How well the solution addresses the problem and benefits the community (Mera Samadhan).

3. Creativity & Innovation

Originality and thoughtful approach to solving the problem.

4. Feasibility & Implementation

Practicality and how realistically the idea can be implemented or demonstrated.

5. Presentation & Explanation

Clarity of explanation, communication, and ability to discuss limitations or improvements.
